Envisioning the Future

Drafting Your Personal Vision Statement

ESTIMATED TIME: 20 MINUTES  ·  4 SECTIONS  ·  RISE PILLAR: I  ·  YLOS: MY INFLUENCE

How to Use This Worksheet
  1. Move through the sections in order — start broad, then narrow. Don’t jump straight to the vision statement.
  2. Write honestly, not perfectly. A rough, true answer beats a polished, safe one.
  3. Your vision doesn’t have to be world-changing in scale. It has to be yours.
  4. When you finish, add your vision statement to your YLOS — and revisit it as it grows.

Your work saves automatically in this browser as you type.

1

What Breaks Your Heart

Start broad. Look at your community — your campus, your neighborhood, your people.

In your community, what do you see that breaks your heart?

Not the whole world’s problems — one thing you keep noticing, one thing you wish were different.

2

Imagine It Different

What would it look like if that were different? Who would benefit?

Describe the better version in plain terms. Name the specific people whose lives would change.

3

Your Role

What’s your role in making it real?

You don’t have to fix all of it. What’s the part that’s yours — the piece only you, with your story and strengths, are positioned to carry?

4

Your Vision Statement

Now bring it together into two lines. This becomes the first draft of your Personal Vision Statement.

Example

“I believe in a world where first-generation students never have to navigate college alone. My leadership exists to build the mentorship and community I wish I’d had.”

Don’t over-polish. This sentence will keep growing as you do.

📋

Add to Your YLOS — My Influence

Add the following to the My Influence section of your YLOS:

  • Personal Vision Statement — your two-line draft, in your own voice
  • Your ‘Why’ — one sentence connecting your background to your purpose
