Scenario Practice
The Deadline No One Will Name

You're part of a four-person group project, and one teammate — someone you actually like — keeps turning their section in late. Each time, the rest of you quietly absorb the cost: staying up to stitch their part in, covering in front of the professor, saying nothing.

The tension is building, but no one has named it. You have no authority over this person — you're equals. But the grade is yours too, and the next deadline is in a week.

Apply → SBI + PEER LEADERSHIP
Step 1 — Read Your Pattern
Step1
→ When tension like this builds, what is your honest first instinct here?
There's no shame in your default — naming it is how you get a choice. Tap the one that's most honest, then write where it comes from and what the silence is already costing the team.
Tap your honest default:
Avoid Attack Address

Step 2 — Build Your SBI Opening
Step2
→ Draft the three parts of your opening. Facts, not labels.
Stay in the moment, not the pattern. Describe what you could film — not what you assume about their character.
S — Situation
One specific moment
B — Behavior
What you observed
I — Impact
The effect it had
Step 3 — End With an Invitation
Step3
→ How will you end so it opens a conversation, not a verdict?
A real question hands them a way back in — and often surfaces something you didn't know was going on. Ask, don't accuse.
0 characters
Step 4 — Lead Your Peer
Step4
→ You hold no authority here. Which move will you lead with?
Among equals, influence is built, not assigned. Tap your move, then write how you'll use it — especially how you'll point to the goal you both care about, not just your preference.
Tap the move you'll lead with:
Credibility Trust Shared Purpose
